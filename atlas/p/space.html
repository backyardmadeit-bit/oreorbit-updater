Critical minerals for the space industry

Satellites and launchers depend on a handful of critical minerals, and for several of them one country controls the key stage of supply. Here is which ones, what they are used for, and how much of Europe's imports come from China.

Key questions

Which critical minerals does the space industry depend on?

Satellites and launchers rely on gallium and germanium for solar cells, rare-earth magnets in reaction wheels, actuators and motors, titanium for structures and fuel tanks, nickel and cobalt superalloys for rocket engines, and lithium for batteries.

Why is gallium important for satellites?

Gallium arsenide is used in the high-efficiency solar cells that power most satellites. 99% of world primary gallium was produced in China in 2024 (USGS MCS 2026).

How dependent is Europe on China for these minerals?

Across the ten index minerals OreOrbit tracks, China supplied 32% of the value of the EU's imports from outside the EU in August 2026 (OreOrbit Dependency Index, Eurostat data). The share varies widely by mineral; see the table above.
