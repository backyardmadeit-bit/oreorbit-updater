Monday brief · week of 19 Oct 2026

China's export-control pause ends in 18 days, and Europe's new supply is still years away

Beijing's pause on its October 2025 controls on rare earths, battery materials and graphite anodes is due to end on 10 November. China has extended its wider trade arrangements with the US to 10 January 2027 but has not said whether that pause goes with them.

  • Gallium: Metlen plans 50 t a year in Greece, with commercial output from Q3 2027. Gallium now sells for about $3,500/kg, against under $500 when the company decided to invest. Investing.com, 21 Oct
  • EU policy: the European Parliament voted 470 to 83 to strengthen the Critical Raw Materials Act, with an EU raw-materials centre backed by a proposed €2 billion fund and powers to restrict exports of magnet scrap. Negotiations with member states come next. Eunews, 20 Oct
  • Nickel: the EU signed a raw-materials partnership with New Caledonia, which produced 3.1% of the world's nickel in 2024. Eunews, 20 Oct
  • US finance: JPMorgan says it has financed or facilitated $230 billion for security-related sectors in a year, including a $75 million, 2.7% stake in Perpetua's Idaho antimony project and $1 billion of financing for MP Materials' magnet expansion. Fortune, 21 Oct
  • Prices: lithium and tungsten have given back most of their 2026 rally. Chinese lithium carbonate futures fell to about half their May peak on 12 October, and tungsten products are down nearly 70% from their highs. MINING.COM (Reuters) and China Tungsten Industry Association, 12 Oct

What it means for Europe

For European buyers, the next two months turn on Beijing's decisions about 10 and 27 November. None of Europe's new capacity arrives before then, with Metlen's gallium due in Q3 2027 at the earliest, so stock levels and export-licence approvals matter more than new projects in the short term. Lithium and tungsten are back near late-2025 price levels, which makes building stocks of those two cheaper than it was in the spring.

  • 15 Nov: applications close for Indonesia's 2027 nickel mining quotas.
  • 27 Nov: China's suspended ban on gallium, germanium and antimony exports to the US is due to resume.

          2030 targets

          Against the EU's 2030 targets.

          The Critical Raw Materials Act sets four goals for each strategic raw material. The cap that matters most: no more than 65% of EU supply from any one country outside the EU. Where one country already makes more than 65% of world output at a stage, Europe can only meet it by buying from the rest of the market or building that stage at home.

          How it stays current

          Every morning an automated process searches for new stories and deadlines and adds them with their links. Every Monday another writes the brief. Sites light up on the globe when they were in the news in the last 14 days.

          Live layer: satellite positions are computed in your browser with the SGP4 model (satellite.js) from CelesTrak orbit data. Earthquakes come from the USGS and disaster alerts from GDACS (UN and European Commission). A scheduled task refreshes them every 3 hours.

          Mine images: Copernicus Sentinel-2, found through Earth Search by Element 84 and Microsoft Planetary Computer, with one clear picture a year since 2017 for the time-lapses. Radar: Sentinel-1, terrain-corrected. Heat: Landsat 8 and 9 surface temperature. Both come through Microsoft Planetary Computer. 3D terrain: Copernicus DEM GLO-30, from TanDEM-X radar data of 2011 to 2015, so it predates recent digging. Globe imagery: NASA Blue Marble and Black Marble; clouds: yesterday's NASA VIIRS true-colour mosaic.

          Since 1986: Landsat 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 surface reflectance, about one clear picture every four years, through Microsoft Planetary Computer. Minerals: NASA's EMIT imaging spectrometer on the International Space Station, mineral identification at 60 m for bare ground only, with vegetation and water masked using Sentinel-2. It shows minerals at the surface, not ore at depth.

          Ships: AIS positions from AISStream.io near chokepoints and ports every three hours, moved on along their reported course and speed for up to an hour; the 3D models are typical of the AIS ship type, not the ship itself. Status trends compare with a weekly snapshot kept every Monday. The Strait of Malacca detour is an atlas estimate from its own sea-lane waypoints.

          Heat and gas: NASA VIIRS 375 m thermal anomalies every day and Sentinel-5P sulphur dioxide every week (NOAA-20 OMPS when Sentinel-5P is unavailable), both read from NASA GIBS map tiles. Trade: Eurostat Comext for EU imports. The stress test scales last year's imports by the months chosen; it does not forecast prices.

          Signals: ship counts refresh weekly, EU imports, tenders and conflict records monthly, all through the same scheduled task. Owners, public funding and the US rule are checked by hand and dated. Water stress, tree-cover loss and research counts are fixed snapshots.
